When something does not work, the usual report is "it doesn't work". With
problem reports switched on, your users can show you instead: they press
**Report a problem**, redo what they did, say what went wrong in a sentence,
and send it. You get the path they took, as steps, on the pages they took it,
and you can replay it in your own app with the teaching cursor.

[Watch it end to end](/demo/report/).

It is not session replay. Nothing is captured until somebody asks for it,
nothing after they press **Done**, and what is captured is a list of controls,
not a film.

## Turning it on

Problem reports are **off for every app** until an administrator turns them
on. In the portal, open the app's **Settings**, find **Problem reports** and
press **Let users report problems**. Two choices come with it:

- **What a report keeps.** *Structure only* is the default and the
  recommended choice: the words on the page are taken out of every step, so a
  report says "Press a button" rather than "Press Order FK-10421". Choose to
  keep the labels only if you know your interface never shows your users'
  own data. Structure only also takes ids out of addresses: `/orders/10421`
  is sent as `/orders/*`.
- **Delete reports after** so many days: 90 by default, anywhere from 7 to
  365. After that a report is deleted outright, not hidden.

## What your users see

Once it is on, the launcher ends with **Something not working? Report a
problem**. Pressing it opens an orange panel, unlike the teal guide, so nobody
wonders which is which.

1. **Redo it.** Each thing they press is added to the list, already masked.
   They can leave any step out.
2. **Say what went wrong.** One sentence, in their own words.
3. **Send it.** The list they approved is exactly what is sent. If it cannot
   be sent, they are told and can try again; closing the panel sends nothing.

following waits, with its progress kept.

## From your own help menu

If you keep the launcher off, offer reports from your own interface:

```js
Wendmark.reportProblem()
Wendmark.reportProblem({ userRef: currentUser.id })
```

`userRef` is optional: an identifier you already have, so whoever reads the
report can reply to the person who sent it. Wendmark stores it exactly as
given and never looks inside it. Without it, a report can be read but not
answered, which is your call to make. It is also how you delete everything one
person has sent.

`reportProblem()` does nothing unless reports are switched on for the app.

## What is never captured

- **Nothing anyone types.** It uses the same recorder as authoring, which never
  reads a field's value, a keystroke or `contenteditable` content.
- **No screenshots, no page content, no console, no network traffic.**
- **Nothing before they start, nothing after they stop.**
- **No identity**, unless you pass a `userRef` yourself.

The portal does not take the snippet's word for any of this: it applies your
app's own masking again before it stores anything. The full reasoning, and the
one gap masking cannot close (test ids built from record ids), is in
[the decision record](/decisions/problem-reports/) and the
[trust model](/running/security/).

## Reading reports

Each app's page in the portal has a **Reports** button with a count.

<Shot
  name="portal-report"
  address="portal.example"
  alt="One problem report in the Wendmark portal: the customer's note, when and from where it arrived, the pages, how it was kept and when it will be deleted, a Replay in the app button, and the masked steps."
  caption="One report: their words, where it came from, and what they did."
/>

- **The list** shows each report's first line, how many steps, the pages, when
  it arrived and, if your app passed a `userRef`, who sent it. Newest first.
- **A report's page** shows the person's note in full, where it came from,
  the pages, how it was kept and the day it will be deleted, then every step
  as it was stored: under structure only, "Press a button" on which page, of
  what kind, whether it pointed at the user's own data, and how the control
  is found again.
- **Delete** removes one report outright, after asking once.
- **Everything from one person**: from a report's page, follow "everything
  they sent" to see one `userRef`'s reports, where an administrator can
  delete them all at once. That is the answer to "delete what you have about
  me".

Administrators and editors can read and delete reports; only an
administrator can erase everything from one person. Viewers do not see
reports at all, since they hold your users' own words.

A report past its retention date is never shown: expired reports are swept
before every read, as well as when new ones arrive.

## Replaying a report

Reading "Press a button, then Follow a link" only goes so far. On a report's
page, **Replay in the app** opens your app and walks the teaching cursor along
the path the person took, the same cursor your guides use.

<Shot
  name="replay-intro"
  closeUp
  alt="The teal guide panel in Fika at the start of a replay. It says this is a replay of a problem report: the steps they took, not necessarily the right ones, and quotes the customer's note, above the three steps."
  caption="A replay starts by saying what it is, and quotes their note."
/>

- **It opens where they were:** the address the report came from, on the
  first page they were on. If that page's address had an id masked out
  (`/orders/*`), it starts at your app's front page instead.
- **You do the clicking,** in your own account. Wendmark points at each step
  and moves on when you have done it, exactly as a guide does.
- **Your data is not theirs.** If a step points at something you do not have,
  such as their order, the replay says it cannot find it and Skip moves on.
  That difference is often the bug.

<Shot
  name="replay-step"
  alt="Fika during a replay: the teaching cursor rings the Add to cart button the customer pressed, and the guide panel in the corner shows the replay's steps."
  caption="The cursor points where they pressed. Support follows."
/>

Opening a replay works like recording: your app asks the portal for permission
and the portal grants it because you, signed in, pressed the button. What it
grants is narrower: reading **that one report**, for fifteen minutes, from that
address. It cannot read other reports or save anything. It needs the same
permission as opening the report, so viewers cannot. [The reasoning is in the
decision record](/decisions/problem-reports/).
