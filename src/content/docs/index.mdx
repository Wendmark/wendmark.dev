Record a process once. Everyone else replays it as guidance: a teaching cursor
glides to the next thing to click, a panel shows where they are, and **the
person clicks it themselves**.

## Why another one of these

Most onboarding tools are built to drive. They click, they fill, they submit —
and the user ends up somewhere they do not know how they got to. Wendmark takes
the opposite position, and it is not a preference but a constraint the codebase
enforces.

<div class="wm-rule">
  <p>Wendmark guides. It never acts.</p>
  <p>
    No synthetic clicks. No setting values. No submitting forms. No navigating
    on someone's behalf. Overlays are <code>pointer-events: none</code>,
    listeners are passive and capture-phase and never call
    <code>preventDefault</code>. A step advances because the person did the
    thing.
  </p>
</div>

## What it never records

The recorder reads structure and the labels your own designers wrote — roles,
accessible names, test ids, selectors. It does not read **anything anyone
types**: not `.value`, not the text content of a field, not `contenteditable`,
not keystrokes. Password, email and payment fields get no special case,
because there is no case to make.

Three things hold that up, and all three are things that *do not happen*: the
recorder never reaches for a value, a test fails if anything touches the value
getter, and the schema rejects unknown keys server-side. There are no
screenshots and no session replay — [we turned those down on
purpose](/decisions/step-snapshots/).

[The full trust model](/running/security/), including what it does **not**
protect, is written down rather than summarised.

## One script tag

```html
<script src="https://your-portal.example.com/snippet.js" data-app="wm_…"></script>
```

That is the whole integration. The snippet is **12.6 KB gzipped** against a
30 KB budget enforced in CI, renders everything inside a shadow root with
`:host { all: initial }`, exposes exactly one global, and uses no framework —
so it cannot restyle your pages and your pages cannot restyle it.

Wendmark adds nothing to your interface unless you ask. The launcher button is
**off by default**; call `Wendmark.openLauncher()` from your own help menu, or
turn it on in the portal and name it whatever suits your product.

## Open source, and self-hostable

Two licences, chosen for where each part ends up running. The snippet is
**Apache-2.0**, because it runs inside other companies' products and nothing
should be asked of their source. The portal is **AGPL-3.0**, because it is the
part someone could host as a service.

There is no contributor licence agreement and none is planned, so what anyone
contributes cannot quietly be made proprietary later.
